A Catalyst switch logs <i>%SW_MATM-4-MACFLAP_NOTIF</i>. What does it tell you, and what do you check?	The same MAC keeps moving between two ports in a VLAN: a sign of a Layer 2 loop.<br>Check STP on every switch carrying that VLAN (is it enabled and working?).<br>Common causes: STP disabled, a load balancer or virtual switch bridging two ports, a user's dumb switch or hub.<br>(pp 67-68)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
What does root guard do when a superior BPDU arrives, and where do you put it?	Puts the port in <b>root inconsistent</b> state for that VLAN (acts like listening, forwards nothing), so it can never become a root port. Recovers by itself when the superior BPDUs stop.<br>Put it on designated ports toward switches that should never be root.<br><i>spanning-tree guard root</i><br>(p 68)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
Two benefits of STP portfast, and what happens if a portfast port receives a BPDU?	1. The port skips listening/learning and forwards right away (good for DHCP, PXE).<br>2. No TCNs from host ports.<br>If a BPDU arrives, portfast is removed from that port and it goes through the normal states.<br>Portfast = RSTP <b>edge</b> port.<br>(p 68)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
Portfast commands: one port, all access ports, turn it off on one port, a trunk to a single host?	<i>spanning-tree portfast</i><br><i>spanning-tree portfast default</i> (global)<br><i>spanning-tree portfast disable</i> (interface)<br><i>spanning-tree portfast trunk</i> (only for one host, e.g. a hypervisor server)<br>(pp 68-69)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
A BPDU arrives on a port with BPDU guard. What happens? (DIKTA Q4)	A syslog message (%SPANTREE-2-BLOCK_BPDUGUARD) and the port goes <b>err-disabled</b>: no traffic at all.<br>Global: <i>spanning-tree portfast bpduguard default</i><br>Per port: <i>spanning-tree bpduguard {enable | disable}</i><br>Check with <i>show interfaces status</i> or <i>show interfaces status err-disabled</i>.<br>(pp 70-71)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
How do you bring BPDU guard ports back automatically, and what is the default timer?	<i>errdisable recovery cause bpduguard</i><br><i>errdisable recovery interval</i> 30-86,400 s (default 300 s).<br>Check with <i>show errdisable recovery</i>. If the switch is still connected, the port recovers and err-disables again.<br>Lab: a port already err-disabled keeps its old timer.<br>(pp 71-72)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
BPDU filter: global vs per-interface behaviour?	Global (<i>spanning-tree portfast bpdufilter default</i>): portfast ports send 10-12 BPDUs when they come up, and filtering stops if a BPDU is received.<br>Interface (<i>spanning-tree bpdufilter enable</i>): no BPDUs sent or processed at all.<br>Book: risky, most designs don't need it.<br>(pp 72-73)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
Why can a unidirectional fiber link cause a loop?	One strand breaks, so line protocol stays up but BPDUs stop arriving. The downstream switch times out its root port, picks a new one, and the blocked port starts forwarding out the strand that still works: a loop.<br>Fixes: loop guard or UDLD.<br>(p 73)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
What does STP loop guard do, how is it enabled, and where should it NOT go?	If a root or alternate port stops getting BPDUs, it goes <b>loop inconsistent</b> (BKN*, *LOOP_Inc) instead of becoming designated. It recovers when BPDUs return.<br><i>spanning-tree loopguard default</i> or <i>spanning-tree guard loop</i><br>Not on portfast ports.<br>See it with <i>show spanning-tree inconsistentports</i>.<br>(p 74)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
UDLD normal vs aggressive, and the main commands?	Normal: no reply means undetermined, the port stays up.<br>Aggressive: 8 more packets at 1 s; no reply means the port goes into an error state.<br><i>udld enable [aggressive]</i> (global, SFP ports), <i>udld port [aggressive]</i>, <i>udld port disable</i>, <i>udld recovery [interval]</i> (default 5 min).<br>Both ends need UDLD. Check with <i>show udld neighbors</i>.<br>(p 75)	ch03 pp67-78 stp
