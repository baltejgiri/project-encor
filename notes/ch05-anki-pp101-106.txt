Name the three switchport modes that can form a trunk with DTP. What does each do, and which is the default on Catalyst?	<b>Trunk</b> (<code>switchport mode trunk</code>): static trunk, and it still sends DTP.<br><b>Dynamic desirable</b>: acts as access but <b>sends</b> and listens for DTP, so it actively asks to trunk.<br><b>Dynamic auto</b>: acts as access and only <b>responds</b> to DTP.<br><br>Default on Catalyst: <b>dynamic auto</b>.	ENCOR ch05 DTP
Two switches are connected. Which DTP mode combination does <b>not</b> form a trunk?	<b>Dynamic auto + dynamic auto</b>: both only listen, so nobody starts the negotiation and the link stays access.<br><br>Every other combination of trunk, desirable and auto forms a trunk.	ENCOR ch05 DTP
What does <code>switchport nonegotiate</code> do, and what is the best practice for switch-to-switch links?	It stops the port sending DTP. A static trunk with nonegotiate <b>won't form</b> a trunk with a dynamic desirable or dynamic auto neighbour. <code>show interfaces &lt;id&gt; switchport</code> shows "Negotiation of Trunking: Off".<br><br>Best practice: set both ends to a <b>fixed mode</b> (<code>switchport mode access</code> or <code>trunk</code>), not dynamic.	ENCOR ch05 DTP
You add a second cable between two switches to double the bandwidth. Why doesn't it work, and how does EtherChannel fix it?	<b>STP blocks</b> one of the links to prevent a loop.<br><br>EtherChannel (port channel, IEEE <b>802.3ad</b>) bundles the member interfaces into <b>one logical link</b>. STP sees only the logical link, so all members forward and traffic is load-balanced across them.<br>It works for Layer 2 (access/trunk) or Layer 3 (routed).	ENCOR ch05 EtherChannel
What are the two main benefits of an EtherChannel?	1. <b>More bandwidth</b>: the total of all active member links.<br>2. <b>Fewer topology changes</b>: if one member link fails, STP and routing don't recalculate, as long as at least one member is still up.	ENCOR ch05 EtherChannel
What is the big weakness of a static EtherChannel (<code>mode on</code>)? Give the DWDM example.	There's <b>no health check</b>. If a link breaks somewhere in the middle (e.g. between two DWDM boxes), the switch ports stay up, so the switches keep load-balancing traffic onto the dead member and you get packet loss.<br><br>LACP or PAgP would detect the failure and take that member out of the bundle.	ENCOR ch05 EtherChannel
PAgP vs LACP: which is the open standard, and what are each protocol's two modes?	<b>PAgP</b>: Cisco proprietary (came first). Modes: <b>auto</b> (only responds) and <b>desirable</b> (initiates).<br><b>LACP</b>: open standard (802.3ad). Modes: <b>passive</b> (only responds) and <b>active</b> (initiates).<br><br>All members must use the <b>same protocol</b> on both ends.	ENCOR ch05 EtherChannel LACP PAgP
Which EtherChannel mode pairs form a bundle, and which don't?	Form: <b>desirable + auto</b>, <b>desirable + desirable</b> (PAgP); <b>active + passive</b>, <b>active + active</b> (LACP).<br><br>Don't form: <b>auto + auto</b>, <b>passive + passive</b> (nobody initiates). Mixing PAgP with LACP doesn't form either.	ENCOR ch05 EtherChannel
