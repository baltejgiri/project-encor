What is process switching, and which three kinds of packets get punted to the CPU?	The router's general-purpose CPU switches the packet (the <code>ip_input</code> process). It's also called software switching or the slow path, and it's the fallback when CEF can't handle a packet.<br><br>Punted packets:<br>1. Packets <b>to or from the router itself</b> (control traffic, routing protocols)<br>2. Packets <b>too complex</b> for the hardware (e.g. IP options)<br>3. Packets missing information (e.g. an <b>unresolved ARP</b> entry)	ENCOR ch01 forwarding-arch
What are the two components of CEF, and what is each one built from?	<b>FIB</b> (Forwarding Information Base): built from the <b>routing table (RIB)</b>, mirrors it, and holds the next hop for each prefix.<br><b>Adjacency table</b> (AIB): built from the <b>ARP table</b> (or other Layer 2 tables). It holds the next-hop IP, the next-hop MAC and the egress interface MAC.	ENCOR ch01 CEF dikta-miss
A packet arrives and CEF checks its tables. What happens if the <b>FIB entry</b> is missing, vs if the <b>adjacency entry</b> is missing?	Missing FIB entry = <b>glean adjacency</b>: the packet goes to the CPU because CEF can't handle it.<br>Missing adjacency entry: ARP is triggered. Once ARP resolves, the full CEF entry is built.<br><br>CPU-bound packets are rate-limited so they can't starve the CPU.	ENCOR ch01 CEF
CAM vs TCAM: what is the difference, and what format are TCAM entries stored in?	CAM is <b>binary</b> (match or no match) and holds the MAC address table.<br>TCAM is <b>ternary</b>: match, no match, or <b>X = don't care</b>. It can match on many fields at once (L2/L3 addresses, protocol, QoS markings).<br><br>TCAM entries use <b>VMR</b>: Value, Mask, Result.<br>Because TCAM is hardware, an ACL runs at the same speed with 10 entries or 500.	ENCOR ch01 TCAM
Centralized vs distributed forwarding: where is the forwarding decision made, and which one gives more port density and scalability?	<b>Centralized</b>: the forwarding engine is on the <b>route processor (RP)</b>, so every packet goes to the RP.<br><b>Distributed</b>: each <b>line card</b> has its own forwarding engine. Packets go straight to the egress line card across the switch fabric, bypassing the RP (dCEF).<br><br><b>Distributed</b> gives more port density and scalability.	ENCOR ch01 forwarding-arch
What is an SDM template, how do you change it on a Catalyst 9300, and what are the gotchas?	SDM (Switching Database Manager) templates set how the switch splits its limited <b>TCAM</b> memory between tables (MACs, routes, ACLs...).<br><code>sdm prefer {vlan | advanced}</code>, then <code>reload</code> (it only applies after a restart). Check it with <code>show sdm prefer</code>.<br><br>Gotchas: every switch in a stack needs the same template. IPv6 entries take double the space. When a table fills up, the overflow goes to the CPU and hurts performance.	ENCOR ch01 SDM TCAM
